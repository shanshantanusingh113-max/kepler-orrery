The space station circles Earth every 92 minutes, about 420 km up. Just after sunset or before sunrise it catches the sunlight while the sky below is dark, and it glides across as one of the brightest lights in the sky. Here is where it is now, and when it will fly over your city.

Orbit data: the latest two-line element set (TLE) from CelesTrak (backup: wheretheiss.at), propagated in your browser with the SGP4 model (satellite.js). Accuracy is about a minute for passes a few days ahead; the ISS sometimes fires its engines, so check again on the day. A pass counts as visible when the station is sunlit, your sky is dark (Sun below −6°) and it climbs above 10°. Map: Natural Earth via world-atlas.
