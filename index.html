The solar system, computed.

The orbit chart on this page is not an animation. It is today's sky, solved from orbital elements on every frame. The same approach powers four research tools: one for spotting changes on planetary surfaces, one for stress-testing rover designs, one for choosing landing sites, and one for checking whether a surprising measurement is real.

M = E − e·sin E

Kepler's equation links where a planet should be on average (M) to where it actually is on its ellipse (E). It has no exact algebraic solution, so the lab solves it with Newton's method, usually in three or four steps, for every planet, every frame. Everything else in the orrery follows from that answer.
